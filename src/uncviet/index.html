About the Program

Why Vietnamese @ UNC?

Vietnamese is the language of over 100 million people worldwide — and of more than 20,000 of your neighbors right here in North Carolina.

Whether you're drawn by family heritage, career goals, a love of Vietnamese culture, or simply the challenge of a new language, UNC Chapel Hill offers a supportive, research-informed path to real proficiency.

Vietnamese is written in Quốc Ngữ — a Romanized alphabet where diacritical marks indicate both tone and vowel quality. While tones take practice, Vietnamese grammar is elegantly streamlined: words never change form for tense, person, or case. Its vocabulary reflects a layered history — Austroasiatic roots, centuries of Chinese literary influence, and a modern lexicon shaped by French colonialism and global contact.

The UNC Vietnamese Language Program fosters real-world proficiency and humanistic growth, teaching not just what students can do in Vietnamese, but who they can become through it. We serve as a vital hub of connection and belonging for all whose lives touch Vietnam.

Getting Started

Placement & Credit

Who needs placement? If you have any prior knowledge of Vietnamese — home use, high school study, self-study, or exchange — you must take the departmental placement test before enrolling. Students with no background whatsoever may enroll directly in VIET 101 each fall.

Not sure where to start? Try our self-access Vietnamese placement test — background survey, listening, reading, and speaking, about 15–20 minutes. It gives you a preliminary recommendation; a short follow-up conversation with the instructor confirms your final placement.

Take the Self-Placement Test →

How Placement Works

  1. Sign up using the DAMES departmental webform. You will receive test logistics and dates by email.
  2. Take the test. Format and timing are provided in your confirmation. The test assesses reading, writing, and listening comprehension.
  3. Receive your result by email and register for the course that matches your placement level on ConnectCarolina.

Heritage & Background Speakers

Heritage Learners

Grew up speaking Vietnamese at home? You belong here. Heritage and background speakers are a valued part of UNC's Vietnamese program. You may arrive with strong listening and speaking skills alongside a desire to strengthen reading, writing, spelling, tones, and formal registers — and our curriculum is designed with exactly that progression in mind.

What We Focus On Together
  • Literacy & formal registers — spelling, diacritics, tone accuracy, and academic vocabulary, while fully honoring your home variety and dialect.
  • Bridging to content — as your proficiency grows, courses incorporate news media, film, literature, and community-based projects that connect to your background.
  • Task-based learning — real-world assignments like community interviews, media analysis, and cultural projects make your existing cultural knowledge an asset in class.

How to Start

Take the placement test — we will recommend the right entry point based on your actual proficiency, not assumptions about what you "should" know. Heritage speakers commonly place into VIET 102, 203, or higher.

The Icon of @uncviet

Our Mascot: Con Trâu

Con trâu — the water buffalo — is the icon of @uncviet. Like Rameses on the same campus, ours is horned, grounded, and unmistakably itself. Diligent, patient, and communal — the buffalo carries the same qualities as Doing, Becoming, and Belonging, the mission this program lives by.

🌾

The Land

Since the Hùng Kings, the trâu has plowed Vietnam's rice paddies — the living engine behind Vietnam's wet-rice civilization.

✨

The Zodiac

In the Vietnamese zodiac, Sửu (the buffalo) is hardworking, dependable, and calm under pressure — the same qualities it takes to learn a language.

🎨

Đông Hồ Prints

Trâu Sen, a child riding a buffalo through a lotus pond, is an iconic Vietnamese folk-art image.

🕊️

The Sage

Laozi rode a thanh ngưu (blue ox) toward wisdom; in Zen, the ox is the mind itself.

🎬

On Screen

Mùa Len Trâu (2004), Vietnam's Oscar submission, follows the water buffalo through Mekong Delta floods.

🍃

The Craft — Trâu Lá Đa

Fold a jackfruit or banyan leaf into a buffalo, thread a blade of grass through the "neck," and pull — the head nods and calls out nghé ọ. No cost, no instructions — passed down for generations.

"Con trâu là đầu cơ nghiệp."

The water buffalo is the foundation of all prosperity — a well-known Vietnamese proverb. Patient, grounded, and quietly determined: the spirit behind everything we build at @uncviet.
