Pronunciation Notes — Distinguish Dấu Hỏi and Dấu Ngã 🗣 Pronunciation
Hỏi vs Ngã
In the Southern variation, thanh hỏi and thanh ngã are pronounced the same. Therefore, even for native speakers of Vietnamese from the South, it can be difficult to distinguish the two tones. In order to write, people will learn by memory which of these two tones is in a given word. They sometimes use poems as a fun way to help them remember the spelling.

NGHĨ mình ngơi NGHỈ dưỡng thương / TĨNH tâm TỈNH ngộ tìm đường ẩn cư. — THINK I'll REST and recover from my injury / CALM my mind, AWAKE and find a way to retreat.
HỏiNgã
nghỉ (to rest)nghĩ (to think)
tỉnh (to be awake)tĩnh (to be quiet)
củ (root)cũ (to be old)
nửa (half)nữa (more)
rưởi (500 thousand VND)rưỡi (half – when talking about time / 500 thousand VND – when talking about money)
sửa (to fix)sữa (milk)

If you have a Southern accent, it is useful to know the minimal pairs so that you can get the correct spelling. The purpose of this exercise is not to train the pronunciation but to match the spelling: it acknowledges the merging of tones in the Southern accent and teaches some spelling rules to strengthen your writing.

Making “funny” but meaningful sentences that include the minimal pair is one way to remember the pairs. Create your sentences (with a recording, if you wish) and share them with your peers so that we can have a cool collection of valuable materials! The full poem is in Further Reading below.

Culture & Varieties
🏮 Cultural Notes — Types of Markets in Vietnam
Chợ truyền thống
Wet markets are a significant part of Vietnamese shopping culture. They are vibrant, bustling places where locals visit daily to buy fresh produce, meat, seafood, and other essentials. These markets offer a wide variety of goods and provide an opportunity for social interaction among locals. People in Vietnam often go to the market to buy fresh produce on a daily basis.
Chợ cóc
A temporary or small market in a neighborhood that serves the needs of the people there. It usually operates only for a few hours in the morning (for people to buy groceries for the day) or in the evening (for people to buy groceries for dinner).
Chợ phiên
A market that only opens on certain days in the month. It is often found in the countryside or mountainous areas. People from nearby areas bring their produce or goods to “chợ phiên” to sell and to buy their necessities.
Chợ nổi
A floating market, called “chợ nổi” in Vietnamese, is a bustling marketplace on rivers and canals, mostly found in the Mekong Delta. Vendors sell their wares from boats, creating a colorful and lively scene. Boat owners often tie what they sell to the top of a tall pole on the boat so customers can recognize them from a distance. These markets offer a glimpse into traditional Vietnamese commerce and life on the water.
Chợ đêm
“Chợ đêm” or “night markets” are vibrant evening gatherings where streets come alive with vendors. Shoppers can browse stalls overflowing with delicious street food, unique souvenirs, and everyday necessities. Chợ đêm is frequently held on weekends in popular tourist spots.
Siêu thị
Supermarkets are often found in big cities. They are often placed in big malls.
